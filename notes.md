**WAKEFIRE: The First Night, The Sleeping God and The Sleepless Dead**

The god of the sky fell asleep, and the city of Ashkel went with him. Azar, the night watchman, is the only one still awake. Find the blade and the flame the first watch kept for this night, nod off to cross into the dream, wake the people who matter, and climb the ziggurat to find out why the god won't wake up.

**New in 2.10.1: a fix.**

- The Bull's Gate in the ziggurat: after the bronze head was broken, leaving and coming back left an invisible block in the gap where it stood. The gap is clear now.
- Two of the rain-dancer's lines read better.

**From 2.10.0: The Sky.**

- **Weather is a state of the story now, over the whole world at once.** Rain, snow and daylight fall on every street, terrace, cliff and hill. Under a roof there is only the sound of it. It never rains in a dream.
- **The rain.** On the threshing floor of the Olive Terraces a man is asleep on one leg, dressed as a fish, with a cane full of seeds: the temple's rain-dancer, stuck on the eleventh step of twelve since the god lay down. The first time the Wakefire's light crosses his eyelids he tips the cane, and it rains everywhere. While it rains no fire bowl under open sky will take; with no moon, no shadow stays up; what the Seal presses runs back to mud; and the Debt's tablet has run, so the sum is half.
- **Ending it.** The twelfth step wants one beat of the dance drum, as the cane comes up. The drum wants its stick. The drummer took it home, and home is under a table at the Seven Jars. Then lie down by the drum and hit it on the beat.
- **The cold spell.** He puts his back into twelve and dances every cloud off the sky. Snow on whatever faces up, ice where there was water, your breath in front of you, bare ground round every fire and round the Wakefire, and a whole city that went to bed in summer shivering in its sleep. Frozen ground takes no print from the Seal, and a Dragnet is stiff on its line. It ends on the thirteenth step, which nobody dances: two beats, one as the cane comes up and one as it comes down.
- **Day and night.** It is always night in Ashkel until the Dawn Horn has been blown. After the end of chapter two the Horn goes on the item button: blow it under open sky and the god turns over. Morning, for ten of his minutes, which is about three of yours. Nothing that walks the night walks in it. Then the night comes back down.

**From 2.9.2: Under the Bed, and the Seal.**

- **Nothing in the crypt is an animal any more.** The jackals and the Den Mother have gone. What is down there now is under the beds.
- **The Underbed, in the Crypt of the First Watch.** An arm from under a bed: long, grey, with more elbows than an arm needs. It feels its way across the floor to your ankle and takes you under, and under one bed is under all of them: you come out from under a different one, short of a little blood and a few shekels. Three cuts and it lets go. It will not put its hand in torchlight; it comes to the edge of it and waits there, drumming its fingers. Until you carry the khopesh it only drums.
- **What it takes, it keeps.** On a heap of four hundred years of left sandals, in a room that is on no plan. The arm under the third bed will show you the way, whether you like it or not.
- **The first bed.** In the last room of the crypt: the first bed of the First Watch, with four arms under it. A blade rings off the bed; it is the hands that can be cut. Cut enough of them and it gets up on what it has left and comes down where you are standing, three times, and then lies there out of breath.
- **The Seal, in the temple yard.** The temple's own cylinder seal, the size of a barrel, rolling the way between the gate and the bridge by itself and sealing the ground. Once round, and it has printed a spearman, who gets up. From the side he is a man with a spear. From the front he is a line, and a line cannot be hit. Strike the Seal from the front as it comes and it goes back the way it came, over everything it printed, until the end of its beat stops it. Three stops and it is in two halves.
- All nine of the bad dreams of Ashkel are in the game now.

**From 2.9.1: The Watch and the Debt.**

- **The Sleeping Watch, in the gate square.** Two of the night watch were out on their rounds when the city went down. They are still on them, asleep, each holding up a lamp that went out forty nights ago. A blade does nothing: nobody hits a man who is asleep. Walk into one and you are arrested, by a sleeping man, for being out after dark, and you come to in the lock-up. There is a way out; the last prisoner dug it.
- **They walk towards any loud noise.** The watch gong by the drill yard calls them. So does a blade ringing on bronze. And the rule they keep says "anything abroad after dark without a light": lead one of them into a Snuffer and he runs the Snuffer in. Afterwards it is in the lock-up, sitting on the prisoner's lamp.
- **The Debt, on the Road of the Dead.** Three hundred years of clay tablets, stacked in the shape of a man, standing in the road above the wall. It follows. Near enough, it holds out the sum; ignored, it brings the tablet down and adds that on. Broken, it stacks itself back up a tablet taller and dearer; the fifth time it has run out of clay. Or it can be paid, and then it lies down where it stands and stays there.
- **The assessor's vault, at the top of the road.** A plate in the ground before it does not notice a man. It notices a Debt.
- A way out (a door, a stair under a floor) can no longer be stepped clean over when the picture runs slow.

**From 2.9.0: Five Bad Dreams.**

- **Nothing that walks the city at night is an animal any more.** The jackals are off the Olive Terraces and the crabs are off the Sea Cliffs. In their place, and in the two towns, are five kinds of thing nobody has met before. Each is the bad dream of somebody asleep close by, up and walking. Each lives in its own part of the city and nowhere else. Each has one rule to learn and one way to turn it to use.
- **Snuffers, in the gate square.** A bronze bell as tall as a doorway on three legs: what the lamp seller puts his lamps out with, out on its own. It comes once the boy has handed over his key, goes for the nearest flame, stands over it and comes down. On its feet a blade only rings off it. Hit it while it is up on its legs and it goes over on its side; break it there and every fire it took flies home to its bowl. If it comes down over Azar, ring it three times from inside. The boy in the dream knows at once when a fire goes out.
- **Wicks, on the Olive Terraces.** Lamp flames that left their lamps. A blade goes through one and only moves it. One follows whoever comes near and now and then goes straight through him. Bring it close to a cold fire bowl and it goes home, and lights the bowl. It goes through bars, which is the only way into the oil store in the corner of the lowest terrace.
- **The Dragnet, on the Sea Cliffs.** Most of the nets on the beach are nets. The ones with money in them stand up, throw themselves, and haul their catch for the sea. Three cuts or one touch of the Wakefire gets him out. A throw that misses leaves it open to a blade. Abu-Nur, the baker and the oracle now say what really took the Night Oil.
- **Stay-Ups, in the Lower Town.** Shadows with nobody to throw them. One takes hold of Azar's shadow, not of him, and his feet stop answering. It keeps out of lamplight. A blade goes through it. The Wakefire held out burns it, and the third time it is gone for good.
- **The Yawn, in the Lower Town.** A patch of bent air going from sleeper to sleeper. Each one yawns as it passes. If it goes through Azar he yawns and can do nothing else; three, and he is asleep on his feet, in the dream. The Wakefire ends it. The Mirror Shield sends it back, and what it goes through on the way falls asleep.
- Killed, burnt off or sent home, each of them stays gone: the towns get quieter as you go.
- A sword blow now lands on its moment even if the picture stutters past it, and the first time one of these is explained to you it waits where it is until you have read it.

**From 2.8.3: The Houses of the Dead.**

- **The ghost city, built again.** The tombs on the Road of the Dead and the Hill of Sleepers were three kinds of box with a painted door. They have come down, and nine houses stand in their place, each its own: a brick vault with the cleanest step on the hill, a long low tomb with a flock cut along its front, a stepped tomb built a little taller than its neighbours on purpose, two vaults under one roof line, a rich man's with a porch of alabaster, a vault with a gong at the door, a small stepped tomb with BACK IN FIVE MINUTES chalked on it, a scribe's with his own work cut right round it, and a round one under a beehive of green tile.
- **You can go in, and somebody is home.** Nine rooms, ten of the dead. Up here it is the dead who cannot sleep. Amat is scrubbing a floor that was clean before you were born. A shepherd has been counting sheep since the god lay down, and you have just made him lose his place. A man is standing on a block of stone that he put in his will. A couple married fifty years are still settling whose side is whose. A rich man sits at a table laid for twelve, the only one who came. A man knocks on the inside of his own door, to know that somebody is there. A scribe is on tablet nine of sixty, and there is one person who has to listen. An old night watchman, three hundred years owed, is plumping a pillow that has been stone since they laid him on it.
- **The ways in.** Four doors stand open. The stepped tomb is bricked up: up the steps on the front of it and down the hatch in its roof. The man who knocks answers the gong beside his door. The watchman will not open to the dark: light the fire bowl at his door. The door of the two vaults is held shut from inside, but the dead do not dream: walk in at it asleep, when nobody is behind it, and draw the bolt. And the house whose owner is out is reached by the hole that robbers once dug into the house next door.
- **The hill has the towns' look, in its own colours.** Walls the white of bone and roofs of bronze tile gone green. Straw where the grass was, kept short. Ways of pale stone with a kerb, and a path from every door. Hedges of dead thorn, clipped all the same. One kind of tree, in rows. Graves in square plots. A stone by every door that says who is behind it.
- Asleep, every one of these rooms is empty. After the dawn they have all gone to bed at last.
- **The rest of the world, in the same look.** The Olive Terraces: kept grass, trodden paths, the terrace walls now ledges of the hill's own rock with grass over the lip and steps cut through, one olive in every row, a board at the way in and one at the gate of the Hill. The Sea Cliffs: the same grass and the same tree on the clifftop, shells on the sand in threes, a board at the head of the steps. The temple yard: a lawn either side of the way in both courts, with a kerb of pale stone.
- **The map is one land.** The map page was seven framed pictures on a clay tablet. It is one country now, a tenth the size: the places themselves set where they lie, the sea along the south, the rock of the Hill to the west with the road of the dead climbing it, the rest of the sleeping city inside its wall, and the ziggurat over the temple yard. Where he has not walked yet lies under the night.
- Next: new monsters, each kind only in its own place, and the polish pass; then day, night and weather.

**From 2.8.2: The Two Towns, Dressed.**

- **Both towns stand in grass.** The swept sand is gone. The Lower Town and the square before the Temple Gate are laid out on kept lawn, and every street, lane and door path is warm cut stone, with one kerb round the lot.
- **One town, one look.** Every house in the Lower Town has the same fresh lime wash under a roof of blue-glazed tile. Every house round the gate square has the same walls under red. Each is still its own house, and no two are alike.
- **Hedges, trees in rows, flowers.** Clipped myrtle round the plots. One kind of round tree, planted along the edges of the town and behind the long houses. Pomegranates in fruit in the orchard. Flowers either side of the gates.
- **A board at every door, saying whose it is.** Thirteen of them. The Seven Jars shows six jars: the seventh was the landlord's own, and he drank it. Ilu-Bani's says BY APPOINTMENT TO THE TEMPLE and, a good deal smaller, NO CREDIT. NOT EVEN THE TEMPLE. The goat's house says NOBODY, and has been signed.
- **The square before the gate** is paved in big pale stones either side of the processional way, and the market in brick. The old inner wall closes its south side, as it always did from below.
- The map page shows both towns as they are now.

**From 2.8.1: The Houses of the Gate Square.**

- **Fixed: a saved journey picked on the title screen opened to a black screen.** The journey loaded and the music played, but the dark was never lifted off the picture. It is now, and the saves themselves were never harmed. If a screen is ever left dark with nothing holding it so, the game lifts it by itself.
- **Fixed: the blue fire in the barrow could not be taken.** The bowl it burns in can be looked at as well, and the bowl was answering first. Something of the dream that can be taken now always comes before something that can only be looked at. Awake, the bowl says plainly that its fire has to be taken asleep.
- **A Climb button.** At the foot of every stair and ladder, and back at the head of one, the action button says Climb. One press and he goes the whole way by himself, up or down. Walking on to the foot still works, and is more forgiving about where you stand.
- **The square before the Temple Gate, built again.** Ten houses that were all one house have come down, and five stand in their place, each its own, each on its own ground. The watch house, with a tower at one corner, the watch fire on top of it, and the rule of the watch cut over the door; beside it the drill yard, with the pell the watch hits instead of each other and a butt with three arrows in it and forty in the wall behind. The house of Ilu-Bani, who sold the Temple its cedar: piers down the front like the Temple's own (the Temple minded), a porch on two pillars, rooms above with round-headed windows, and a walled garden with a pool. The lamp seller's, with a lamp burning in every niche of its front. The tablet house, where the sons of Ashkel learn to write and wish they were dead, today's lines drying on the roof in rows. And Widow Ilta's, blue at the door, her bed on the roof, and in the yard the fig tree she planted the week her husband went out for figs. To save him the trip.
- **Twelve rooms.** The guardroom, the tower room over it and the lock-up under it. Ilu-Bani's cellar, his hall with a pool let into the floor, and the rooms above. The schoolroom, and next to it the room where the master keeps what he takes off the boys. Widow Ilta's kitchen, with a table laid for two. The lamp seller's shop, the brightest room in Ashkel, and under it the oil cellar, which he keeps dark on purpose. You are carrying a flame. Walk like a cat.
- **Somebody is home.** Fourteen sleepers, a hound, a cat, a tortoise and a frog. One of the day watch is face down on the game of twenty squares, one throw from winning; one is asleep at attention; one was off duty and in his bunk when it happened, and wants to know whether this counts as his rest day. Ilu-Bani sleeps on his feet with one hand flat on his strongbox. His daughter has a list under her pillow of what he sold the Temple and what he told them it cost. The master of the tablet house has had the stick raised for forty nights; the boy under it has had time to think. A third boy was sent to the corner and has not been told he may turn round.
- **The ways in.** The watch house stands open. Ilu-Bani's front door is barred from inside, by a man who has been robbed before. The watch's one prisoner spent a year on a tunnel with a spoon, came up in a cellar full of cedar and silver, and thought he would just rest his eyes: the cell keys are on a peg within reach of the cell, and from inside the merchant's house the bar lifts out. The lamp seller's door opens when his sign is lit, and his sign is the one flame on the front of his house that is out. The tablet house has a ladder, a roof, a hatch, and a grille the boys have looked through for years, which unbolts from the far side. And Widow Ilta's house has one more room than it has when you are awake.
- **The market keeps the east side**, paved in stone setts now, with the well in its own court of tiles. Widow Ilta still walks her round. The goat is still awake.
- In the Lower Town, the people who are asleep in beds lie along them, instead of across.

**From 2.8.0: The Houses of the Lower Town.**

- **Every house is its own house.** The Lower Town has been pulled down and built again: eight buildings, no two alike, each on its own walled plot with a paved path from its door to the street. Hesha's bakery with its hatch of loaves and the bedroom over the shop. The Seven Jars, long and low, with a roof to drink on. The weavers', with forty nights of dry cloth still hanging from the gallery. The counting house, in dressed stone to the height of a man, because the first two were of brick. Ummi the oracle's, under a beehive dome. Kesh's pottery, with the loft he keeps the sacred jars in. The net loft on the quay, roofed with the hull of a boat. And the house the goat stands on.
- **You can go in.** Fifteen rooms behind those doors, each its own screen with its own walls, floor and furniture: the shop, the room over it and the flour cellar under it; the hall of the Seven Jars and what is underneath; the looms and the sisters' room; the strongroom; the room the oracle keeps for answers.
- **Somebody is home.** Eleven sleepers nobody has met before, a second dog, and a cat. Hesha's husband, asleep on his feet with a coin in each hand: he was counting the takings and had got as far as four. The weavers, who are sisters, at looms as far apart as the room allows, with a line chalked down the floor of the room they share. The collector's clerk, face down in the tablets. A client on the oracle's mat who has not liked her answer once in forty nights. Each has a dream-self, and each of those has something to say.
- **The outside stairs go somewhere.** The stair up the front of the bakery and the one at the end of the Seven Jars are stairs now: walk on at the foot and Azar climbs to the roof. Ladders too. A roof is a place: walk about on it, go in at the door up there, and leave by whichever edge is nearest. He hops down.
- **Some doors are shut.** The counting house opens to its gong, for people who have come to pay. The weavers bolted their street door ten years ago and come and go by the roof, like the cats; so can you. The goat's house is barred from inside, by somebody who then left through the roof. The board behind the counter of the Seven Jars is bolted from underneath: the way under is the cellar door in the alley, or the hole at the foot of the sea cliffs that the crabs use.
- **Things worth finding.** The goat keeps a key. The collector keeps a strongroom. The potter walled up his own cash room from the inside and has to go in through a hatch in the roof; the plaster is still soft in one place. Under the flour bin in the bakery there is a stair. And in the oracle's house there is a door that is only there when your eyes are shut.
- **A tidy town.** Streets paved and kerbed, a tiled court round the well, an orchard, gardens in beds, a lamp at every gate, hedges of clipped myrtle. One kit of walls, roofs, doors and windows for the whole town, so that it belongs together; and no house in it that could be mistaken for the one next door.

**From 2.7.0: What the Plans Do Not Show.**

- **Floors above the floor.** Rooms in all four dungeons have a higher level now. In the Crypt of the First Watch the locked door stands on a terrace a tile above the hall, at the head of its own flight of steps. So do the stair of the ziggurat's Stair Hall, the Great Door, the stair of the tomb's Pillared Hall, the last of the four fires on the Brink, and the king's own door; the Herdsman's stair and the head of the barrow's passage are flights from wall to wall. The edge of a raised floor is a ledge: nothing walks off it or on to it, and what is thrown goes over it. Lean on one from above and Azar hops down. The way back up is the steps.
- **Seven rooms that are on no plan.** A dungeon's scroll does not show them. Your own feet do, once they have stood in one; and a tuning fork that hears a chest through a wall draws the room it heard it in. Each has its name, and whatever somebody was keeping there.
- **Walled-up doorways.** A crack runs down the wall, and a cut rings hollow on it. The third blow of three, the one with his whole weight behind it, brings the wall down on both sides, for good. So does the Sun Wheel. In the ziggurat one hides the Twelfth Room, which the temple's plans leave out because the priest who drew them had it built; another, the Oil Store, walled up when the Herdsman was found to be drinking it. In the king's tomb the queen had a room of her own walled off the week she was buried, and paid the mason twice: once for the wall, and once to forget where it was.
- **A stair that waits.** Under the floor of the ziggurat's Counting Room there is a stair, and it is not there until nobody is left to see who uses it. It goes down to the Twelfth Room. Come up it from below and the slab opens for you.
- **The oil chute.** A hole in the floor of the High Landing: *jars only, not acolytes, not acolytes again*. A hole with light coming up it has a floor under it. Lean on the lip and he jumps, and comes down on the sacks in the Oil Store, one floor below.
- **The way the jackals go.** A hole dug through the foot of the dormitory wall in the crypt. It is exactly as wide as a jackal. Behind it is the watch's old larder, and four hundred years of left sandals.
- **Under the well, a cistern.** The king's well has a floor. Somebody on the staff has been coming down to drink for four hundred years (the dead do not drink; the staff were never told they had died). In the cistern there is a well. A ladder comes out on the side you went in from.
- **Doorways only a dreamer has.** A passage in the Generals' Barrow stops at a wall, with both armies' drums in front of it. Asleep, there is no wall: there is the Gallery, two tiers of mud brick where the off-duty of both armies have sat side by side for three hundred and ninety-one years watching a wall, by order of both generals. Do not hit one. And in the king's tomb a clerk went through a wall to file a request for the key, and filed himself.
- **The map page keeps a secret.** A doorway the wall does not show is not drawn until you have stood on both sides of it.

**From 2.6.0: The Staff of the High Halls.**

- **Medusas.** The doorkeepers of the ziggurat's high halls. The temple took them on because nobody argues with a doorkeeper they cannot look at. A Medusa is found with her back to the room, at her hair with a comb (the hair is against it). She turns, the snakes stand up, the light comes into her eyes, and she looks. Meet her eyes and Azar is stone for as long as the Wakefire takes to burn it off him, and she comes over while he waits. Hold the Mirror Shield up square to her and it is her own eyes she meets: she is stone instead, and stone can be cut. A turned back gives her nothing to meet. Neither does a man asleep on his feet: his eyes are shut.
- **Thurifers.** The temple's censer-bearers. The service stopped forty nights ago and nobody has told them. A thurifer walks his round with the censer going over his head on its chain, and nothing gets inside the circle of it. Every few rounds he lets it out to its full length at whoever is nearest. Mirror Shield up, and it goes home the way it came, round his own neck twice, and he stands coughing in his own incense until he has got it off.
- **Burning glasses.** A lens as wide as a shield in a bronze ring on three legs. The priests lit the high lamps with it from the floor, and nobody has turned it off. It turns to follow whatever moves, gathers the light (you can hear it gather), holds its line, and lets go. Step out of the line, or stand in it with the Mirror Shield up: glass does not care for its own light. Three times, and it stops being a glass.
- **Where they are.** All three keep the 3F High Halls: a Medusa on the Last Stair and another over the Bull Key, a burning glass at the end of the Lamp Way, thurifers in the West Light and before the Great Door. A thurifer walks his round past the bull's head on 2F, and the vault on 1F that only opens to the mirror has a doorkeeper of its own.
- **Two of them went up the hill.** The king had a Medusa sent up from the temple for his own door: she has been on it four hundred years and is still not through the hair. And the thieves carried a burning glass up to open that door with. It opened two thieves and a donkey first, and they left it where it stood. In the tomb the Sun Wheel works on all three: it comes in flat under a thurifer's chain, cracks a glass from across the room, and finds a Medusa from behind.
- **The jackals of the olive terraces, rebuilt from nothing.** A deep chest on long legs, a black saddle, ears like two spearheads, a brush with a black end to it, and eyes that give the lantern back. The Den Mother is the same animal grown old and heavy in the dark: grey down the back, one ear in ribbons.
- **The brine crabs of the sea cliffs, rebuilt from nothing.** A shell gone the green of old bronze with a bright rim and points down each shoulder, eyes on stalks, legs that end in a point, and claws with a thumb that opens before it shuts. The right claw is the bigger. It is always the right one.
- New sounds: the hair, the look, the censer going round and coming home, the glass gathering, letting go, cracking and breaking.

**From 2.5.0: The King's Tomb.**

- **The king's tomb is a real dungeon: three floors of rooms, each in its own shape.** It has a scroll of its plan, a tuning fork of its own (carnelian, the red of old blood), a keeper for its great chest, a boss key that was made for this tomb, and a door at the top that opens to nothing else.
- **1F, the Outer Halls.** The antechamber the thieves got as far as. The servants, dead and on duty, across the one doorway. An offering store with a scale that wants one goat, in gold. A treasury behind a lock whose key is only anywhere in a dream. The lamp walk. The well.
- **2F, the Inner Halls.** The hall of the lyre, two halls long with a cut through it. The bearers' way. The hall of the sun. The sun court. The wardens' hall, the queen's room, and the brink.
- **3F, the King's Road.** A crossing with a bird over it. East: the falconers' way, the king's mews and the roost. West: the clerks' passage and the steward's rest. North: the king's own door.
- **A keeper for the tomb's item: the Sun Roller.** A scarab the size of a cart found the gold the thieves dropped, made a ball of it, and has been rolling it up and down its hall for four hundred years on the understanding that it is the sun. The sparks say which way the sun will go. It banks off two walls and stops at the third. The beetle on top of it does not stop. A beetle on its back is the moment.
- **The Sun Wheel.** A bronze disc with an edge of gold, in a great chest behind the Sun Roller. Throw it with the item button: it goes the way you are walking, straight or across the corners. It banks off the first two walls it meets, and the third sends it home. Press the item button again while it is out and it comes home at once, through whatever is in the way. It rings gongs across a drop, cuts rope, and does not mind what is under it.
- **Enemies for the wheel: the wardens.** The king's clay again, behind a shield the size of a door, and the shield is always between you and him. Nothing gets in from the front. Throw past his shoulder, step into line with him, and call the wheel home: it arrives from behind. They have never once looked behind them.
- **Enemies for the wheel: the king's vultures.** He kept them the way other kings keep hawks. They sit on bronze perches a spear's length up, out of any blade's reach, open their wings at whoever they have picked, and come down in one long stoop. The wheel takes one off its perch. A stoop that meets the raised Mirror Shield ends on the floor, and the floor is where a blade finds it.
- **The lamp bearers.** Dead, and on duty. A blade goes through one like a hand through lamp smoke, and the lamp he carries burns cold. But the dead do not dream. Nod off and he is not there, and his lamp is left hanging where he held it. Knock it out of the air: a bearer with nothing to carry looks at his hands, and is off duty for the first time in four hundred years.
- **The Ghost Key.** The tomb's boss key, and nobody else's: the key to the king's own door, buried with the steward who kept it about his person. The steward is still there. He cannot pick it up either. The dead of the house step aside for whoever carries it: they think you are management. (The ziggurat keeps its Bull Key exactly as it was.)
- **Two new things for Nod's Pocket.** A whole gold goat, off the top of a thief's dream of being rich: the king's offering scale wants exactly one. And the order the king left his servants with four hundred years ago: STAND THERE. Take it out of the dream and nobody has told them anything, so they sit down. Let it out at the king's clay and the clay stands there, for a while. Orders wear off.
- **Puzzles for the wheel.** A gong you only ring by standing on the sun in the floor and throwing at the sunrise. A drawbridge held up by one rope, a long way out for a blade. A counterweight round a corner. A gong on an island. Four fires in four halls for the door with the sun on it.
- The wheel, the vultures, the Ghost Key and the king's door have sounds of their own. A slinger in the barrow now winds up before he throws.

**From 2.4.0: Real Dungeons, and the Generals' Barrow.**

- **No dungeon is a block of rooms any more.** Every floor has been dug again as a real dungeon: halls with their corners built up, passages, wings of different lengths, a room shaped like an L.
- **The crypt:** the watch's walk with a niche off it, a dormitory twice as long (twelve beds, more jackals, a hoard at the far end of their store), and a jackals' run that doubles back round a pier of rock before the den.

- **Android:** download `WAKEFIRE.apk` on your phone and open it. If your phone asks, allow installs from your browser or files app.
- **Google Play:** `WAKEFIRE-Play.aab` is the bundle for the Play Console.

Controls: on a phone, put your thumb down anywhere on the left to walk; the blade button sits under your right thumb with roll, the action button, the item button and nod off around it, and the pause button at the top opens the pack. The blade and the moon button appear when Azar has found the khopesh and the Wakefire. On a computer, use WASD or the arrows to move, J or Space for the khopesh, K to roll, E for the action button (hold it to raise the shield), F for the item, Q to nod off and Esc for the pack. Gamepads work too.

The game saves by itself into the journey you are playing every time you walk into a new place and every time you find something.
